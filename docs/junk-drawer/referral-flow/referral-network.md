# One tree. Two reward paths.

Holdings allocate credits. Families can share referral commissions.

Mayflower’s referral network connects ongoing asset participation with a market for referral relationships. Members earn from eligible holdings. Their ancestors earn defined commissions. Parents can then use some of those commissions to make their families more attractive to their immediate children.

The network has two reward paths, both working within the same scheduled credit budget.

**Holdings determine the core allocation.** Each AVA has its own lane, with an assigned credit budget and a total eligible balance. A member’s proportion of that balance determines the credits attributed to its position. The holder retains part of that attribution, and each earning ancestor receives the network’s fixed commission fraction.

Suppose Dana holds 10% of one lane’s eligible balance during an interval with 3,000 credits allocated to it. Her position is attributed 300 credits. Dana’s parent is Bob, and Bob’s parent is Alice. With a 10% commission per ancestor, Dana retains 240, Bob receives 30, and Alice receives 30. They divide the same 300 credits. Dana’s AVA principal is not spent on these commissions.

A referrer can earn from descendants’ holdings without holding AVAs personally. A member can also earn from its own position without recruiting. The network fixes a maximum depth and a commission fraction that leave even its deepest members a positive share of their own-position rewards. Members at that maximum depth cannot recruit further.

**Parents can share their referral commissions.** A parent chooses how much of its gross referral income to offer to its immediate children. This is the family pool, also called mutualization. It combines referral commissions from all the parent’s asset lanes. The parent’s own-position rewards, admission fees, and incoming family allocations are outside that pool.

Continuing the example, Alice offers 50% of her 30 commission credits. She keeps 15 and shares 15. Bob has 100 previously issued credits in family escrow, while his sibling Carol has 200. Bob receives one-third of the pool: 5 credits. Carol receives two-thirds: 10 credits. Their 300 escrowed credits remain their principal; the pool does not consume them.

After sharing, the allocation from Dana’s position is Alice 15, Bob 35, Carol 10, and Dana 240. The total remains 300. Family sharing changes who receives the allocation without creating another emission stream.

**This gives siblings an interest in each other’s branches.** Dana belongs to Bob’s downline. Carol still receives part of the commission generated by Dana’s holdings through Alice’s family pool. Carol need not have AVA holdings or recruits of her own to receive this family allocation; she needs issued credits in the corresponding escrow.

That is the reason for combining referral ancestry with family sharing. Parents compete through admission prices and sharing offers. Children can gain exposure to participation across their parent’s wider downline. A productive branch can contribute to a pool that benefits siblings beside it.

Credit escrow gives that sharing a proportional weight. Splitting Bob’s 100 credits between two child accounts under Alice would divide his existing share between them. It would not increase the combined share. The relevant commitment is the credits held in escrow. These credits are separate from AVA emission pledges and clearing bids; the same principal cannot back those uses simultaneously.

**The relationship is permanent; the offer and balances can change.** An ordinary member chooses a parent and pays its quoted join fee, which may be zero. That fee belongs to the parent and is separate from the child’s family escrow. Scarce top-level seats instead come from an authorized auction whose winning credit payment is burned. The logical protocol root earns nothing.

A parent can change its sharing percentage for future earnings, including setting it to zero. Children can withdraw family principal after prior earnings are settled, without a mandatory lock or forfeiture. Joining another family requires a new node; an existing parent connection and its descendants stay in place.

The accounting preserves the past. Before balances or sharing terms change, earlier earnings settle under the earlier terms. Received family allocations stay with their recipient; they are not automatically shared onward or added to escrow. Collection issues the net earned credits once, and restaking is a separate choice.

The intended arrangement follows Mayflower’s commitment to permissionless public machinery without operator custody. A referral parent receives defined fees and commissions, not control of a child’s AVAs or escrow principal. The implementation must enforce ownership, settlement, and withdrawal through the public program rules.

Competition takes place within the shared emission budget. New holdings also compete for each lane’s allocation, and new family stakes compete for its pool. Actual earnings therefore depend on participation, lane weights, the emission schedule, and current sharing terms. The ambition is to give people a reason to attract and maintain useful participation, with transparent rules for how that participation is rewarded.
