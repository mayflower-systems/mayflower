Mayflower credits connect reward allocation and revenue clearing.
[Emissions](Credit-Emissions) give eligible participants a way to earn them.
Holders can pledge credits to direct future emission among channels, or spend
them in the [clearing auction](Revenue-Clearing) to claim a portion of funded
revenue.

## Earning and directing credits

The emission schedule determines the available stream, and channel pledges steer
its relative distribution among channels. A pledge uses already-issued credits,
can be reduced, and affects allocation while it is active. It does not increase
the emission schedule. Members earn their channel's allocation through eligible
points and the [referral network](Referral-Network).

Channels compete for channel weight, while members compete for a share of the
allocation within a channel. Additional points change the proportions within
that channel; they do not increase the scheduled stream. Referral overrides
reward productive recruitment, and voluntary mutualization lets parents share
earnings with direct children. These choices divide the same channel allocation.

## Spending credits on revenue

Clearing participants commit credits to claim shares of a funded reward pot.
Remaining principal is irreversibly forfeited at finalization, while an earlier
withdrawal forfeits its applicable penalty. The reward a participant receives
depends on the pot, competing shares, entry time, withdrawals, and rounding.

The clearing auction and emission schedule are separate: spending credits in
clearing does not automatically change the emission rate, and new emission does
not automatically fund a clearing pot.

## The cycle

1. Eligible participation earns credits through emissions and the referral
   network.
2. Holders can pledge credits to direct the distribution of future emissions
   among channels.
3. Revenue can fund a clearing auction, where participants spend credits to
   claim rewards.
4. Scheduled issuance adds to supply when retained rewards are collected;
   clearing forfeitures remove spendable credits. Their combined flows shape
   circulating supply over time.

The schedule's rate can stay flat or decrease. Whether circulating supply rises
or falls also depends on collections, clearing, and other supply changes.
